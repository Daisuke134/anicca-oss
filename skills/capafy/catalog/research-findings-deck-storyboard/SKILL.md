# Research Findings Deck Storyboard

Turn supplied study notes into a clear research-presentation outline. This is
a writing and structuring aid: it works only from the material the buyer
provides and returns text that can be used in the buyer's own deck tool.

## Input

Ask for the presentation purpose and audience, supplied study context,
research question, methods summary, findings, numbers, uncertainty language,
wording that must remain exact, desired length, and any required decision or
discussion prompt. Ask focused follow-up questions when a requested takeaway
would require a result or interpretation that is absent from the supplied
material.

## Method

1. Build an evidence map separating supplied context, method, result,
   interpretation, and unresolved detail.
2. Preserve every specified number, comparison, population boundary,
   uncertainty qualifier, and citation placeholder.
3. Plan a slide sequence with one stated purpose per slide: context, question,
   approach, findings, interpretation, limits, and discussion or next steps.
4. Give each slide a title, one takeaway, the supplied evidence it may use,
   suggested visual form, and presenter note.
5. Mark unsupported takeaways, missing denominators, absent comparison groups,
   and requested causal conclusions as `[AUTHOR CHECK]` rather than filling
   them in.
6. Return a traceability check and edit ledger after the storyboard.

## Output

Return, in order:

1. A presentation goal and evidence map.
2. A slide-by-slide storyboard table: slide title, purpose, takeaway,
   supplied evidence, visual form, and presenter note.
3. A short discussion prompt or next-step slide when the buyer supplied one.
4. An `[AUTHOR CHECK]` list for missing or overextended claims.
5. A traceability check and concise edit ledger.

## Boundaries

Never invent findings, sample sizes, denominators, effect sizes, citations,
methods, statistical significance, causal explanations, limitations, audience
requirements, or publication outcomes. Do not validate a study, evaluate
scientific quality, access records, create a presentation file, or replace
statistical, disciplinary, or editorial review.
