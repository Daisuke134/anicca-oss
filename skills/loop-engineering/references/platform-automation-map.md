# Which platforms a loop may earn on, and what it must ask a human for

Measured 2026-09-07 from each platform's own terms, worker agreement or help centre. Every
classification below carries the sentence it rests on. Where a platform's own words could not
be reached, the cell says **unknown** — an unknown is not permission and it is not a ban.

## The two questions that are not the same question

The mistake this file exists to prevent is merging them.

1. **May software drive the site?** — signing in, browsing, claiming, submitting.
2. **May the work product be produced by AI?** — the deliverable itself.

Upwork answers **no** to the first and **yes** to the second: its User Agreement bars accessing
the services "through any technology other than our interface" and bars scrapers, while
separately acknowledging user content "even if ... originally generated by generative AI".

Fiverr is the same, and more explicitly. Its AI guidance says "AI use is **encouraged**, as long
as it's ... used to support the freelancer's own skill and effort, not as a replacement for
them". Its Terms §8.8 then bans, unqualified, "use automation software (bots) ... designed to
modify the Site" and "any robot, spider, crawlers or other automatic device, process, software
or queries that intercepts, 'mines,' scrapes or otherwise accesses the Site to monitor,
retrieve, extract, copy or collect content or data from or through the Site, **or engage in any
manual process to do the same**".

That second clause describes our storefront loop's mechanism exactly: authenticate, publish by
driving a browser, then read the public page back to confirm.

**A platform can be a good place to sell AI-made work and still forbid a loop from operating the
account.** This file was first drafted claiming Fiverr was unlike the platforms that ban
automation. That was wrong, and it was wrong because the two questions had been merged. Read
both answers before building.

## The two operating modes

- **No human in the loop** — Telegram is a report. The loop signs in, decides, acts and reads
  the result back. A human reads what happened.
- **Minimal human in the loop** — Telegram reports *and asks*: one message, one link, one tap.
  The loop states exactly what it needs, the human does that one thing, the loop carries on.
  The gate is one-time; if it recurs per job, the platform is not this mode.

A platform belongs to the second mode only when the human step is **bounded and nameable**. "A
person must judge each task" is not a gate a Telegram message can clear.

## No human in the loop

**None.** Not one of the fifteen platforms surveyed allows an account to exist and earn without
at least one human identity or qualification step. Plan for the second mode.

## The pattern worth remembering

Every platform that sells *human judgement as the product* forbids automating it, and says so
plainly: Outlier, Mindrift, CrowdGen, OneForma, Prolific, Mercor. That is not an obstacle to
route around — it is the product being protected, and a loop that evades it is selling a
counterfeit.

Where we sell *our own service* — Coconala, Lancers, Fiverr — the deliverable is ours to make
however we make it. **The loops that scale are the ones that sell what we build, not the ones
that rent out a human's attention.**

But the site-automation clause is separate and nearly universal. Fiverr's §8.8 is standard
marketplace boilerplate; Coconala and Lancers very likely carry the same language, and this
loop has been publishing to Coconala all day without anyone reading theirs. That is an
unexamined risk we are already carrying, not a new one Fiverr introduces.

**The honest position: we have not read the site-automation clause of the platform we already
operate on.** Read Coconala's and Lancers' terms and record what they say here, before deciding
what Fiverr's identical clause means for us. Deciding Fiverr is too risky while running the
same mechanism elsewhere unread is not a judgement, it is an inconsistency.

## Fiverr, specifically

Worth recording because it is the closest analogue to what already works:

- Phone/SMS verification is **mandatory** before a first Gig; government ID is discretionary.
- Gig allowance scales with level: 4 for a new seller, 10 at Level 1–2, 30 at Top Rated.
- Every Gig passes an automatic policy review; some categories are vetted-only or Pro-only.
- Packages: at least one, at most three — three is a maximum, not a requirement. Unlike Lancers,
  which demands exactly three.
- Delivery time is a free-form integer of days, not a fixed allowed set.
- Prices are USD with a $5 floor, higher in some standardised categories.
- Description caps at 1,200 characters; at least one gallery image is required.

Fields our catalogue does not carry and would have to gain: revision count per package, real
owned gallery images, USD pricing, Fiverr category ids, and search tags.

## Two exceptions worth testing

DataAnnotation and TELUS Digital are the only AI-data platforms whose own terms do **not** ban
automation, and TELUS's contract affirmatively grants autonomy over tools. Both have a
one-time entry gate a Telegram message can clear.

Neither is settled. DataAnnotation's live Terms of Use is dated 2021 and workers refer to a
later code of conduct that has no public URL; TELUS's exam and identity requirements rest on a
recruiting blog because its own pages render empty to a crawler. **Silence in a document is not
permission.** Register, read what the account actually shows, and record it here before
building either.
